# Education Coding Rules

## Education Coding Scale

| Value | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 | Less than high school diploma or GED. |
| 2 | High school diploma or GED required or actively pursuing. |
| 3 | For teachers, clock hours in ECE, credits, or credential less than CDA. For directors, clock hours in ECE, staff supervision, or human relations, credits, or credential less than CDA. |
| 4 | CDA (120 ECE clock hours). |
| 5 | Clock hours in ECE, credits, or credential more than CDA but below a required associate degree. |
| 6 | Associate degree in ECE/related field, or at least 60 semester credits with substantive required ECE/related coursework. |
| 7 | Bachelor's degree in unrelated field. |
| 8 | Bachelor's degree in ECE or a related field; or a bachelor's degree in any field plus substantive required ECE-specific coursework, certificate, or credential. |

## Coding Rules

1. **Teacher definition.** We define a teacher as the position requiring the least education that can independently supervise a group of children for a full working day. We exclude center-wide curriculum positions but apply higher education requirements when at least 20% of staff needed to meet staff-child ratios must hold those qualifications. We use the largest center size and most staff-intensive age-specific ratio to determine whether this threshold is met.

2. **Director definition.** We define a director as the position necessary to perform the administrative duties for one or more childcare centers.

3. **Minimum-education pathway.** When multiple pathways to qualification exist, we use the pathway requiring the least education, subject to the other coding rules.

4. **Age-specific requirements.** We code minimum education as the highest applicable minimum across ages 0 through 5. For example, if a CDA qualifies an individual to supervise one age group but not another, we use the higher binding education requirement.

5. **Center-size requirements.** When education requirements vary by childcare center size, we use the most stringent minimum applicable to the largest center size that meets our other coding requirements.

6. **Experience requirements.** We do not count experience as an education requirement. However, when the least-education pathway requires more than two years of experience, we use the education requirement of the next available pathway. Requirements of exactly two years, including "at least two years," do not trigger this exception.

7. **Education requirements after hire; annual training; short training.** We count qualifying education requirements that must be completed within one year of hire toward minimum education, except as follows. Annual or otherwise recurring in-service or continuing training requirements are excluded from teacher and director education coding because annual training is coded as a separate variable, even when the first annual training requirement must be completed within one year of hire. We also exclude any standalone training requirement that can be completed in eight or fewer clock hours, regardless of whether it is called orientation or covers substantive topics such as early childhood education, child development, or administration. This exclusion is not limited to orientation. Required nonrecurring education or training exceeding eight clock hours that must be completed within one year of hire remains eligible for inclusion, subject to the other coding rules.

8. **Incomplete education and plans of study.** We do not count mere enrollment, pursuing a credential, working toward a qualification, or beginning a plan of study as completion of the required education or credential. However, when a small portion of a longer educational requirement would likely be completed and change the coding from 2 to 3 or from 4 to 5, we count that progress. Examples include:

   - **Georgia:** We count progress toward a degree that must be completed within 18 months.
   - **Alabama, New Mexico, and North Dakota:** We code the applicable education requirement as 3.
   - **Washington:** We exclude the five-year additional education requirement but code the 30-clock-hour requirement as 3.
   - **Michigan:** We code the program administrator requirement as 5 because it requires education beyond a CDA within 12 months.

9. **Grandfather clauses.** We use the requirements applicable to new staff rather than earlier requirements preserved through grandfather clauses.

10. **Education equivalencies.** For coding purposes, we treat four ECE credits, 120 ECE clock hours, a CDA, two years of high school vocational training, and 12 CEUs as equivalent. Our clock-to-credit-hour conversion is informed by the [U.S. Department of Education's conversion guidance](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2021-05-25/implementation-updated-clock-credit-conversion-regulations-ea-id-general-21-34).

11. **National administrator credentials.** We code approved national administrator credentials based on their required education and training hours, including any additional qualifications required by the state. For example, Texas recognizes a 45-hour National Administrative Credential, which we code as 3.
