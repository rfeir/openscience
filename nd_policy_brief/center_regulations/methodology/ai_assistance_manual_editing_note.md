# AI Assistance and Sourcing Instructions

## AI Assistance and Manual Review

AI tools were used to locate and gather historical regulatory documents, generate preliminary childcare regulation codes according to the project's SCR/MGS and education coding rules, and audit the resulting codes. Each coded observation was manually inspected by the researcher. Manual review was also iterative: observations and supporting legal sources were revisited as ambiguous cases and state-specific requirements emerged. Prompts and coding instructions were refined alongside this review process to improve consistency across states and observation years.

The instructions below consolidate the evidence-collection requirements for reuse. They are **not a verbatim record of every historical prompt**; the research process involved multiple iterations and references to earlier work. The substantive coding criteria are recorded separately in `scr_mgs_rules.md` and `education_coding_rules.md`.

## Evidence Collection and Verification Instructions

Use the accompanying SCR/MGS and education coding rules when researching or reviewing ordinary licensed, full-day, center-based childcare requirements. Identify the regulations legally effective on **July 1, 2013; July 1, 2018; and July 1, 2023**, rather than assuming current rules applied historically. For SCR and MGS, apply the rules to children **exactly 6 months** and **exactly 4 years** old.

1. **Evidence requirements.** For every coded observation, retain:
   - State, year, variable (and age, where relevant), and coded value
   - Governing legal citation
   - Historical source URL or document
   - Short verbatim excerpt of relevant legal language
   - PDF page and/or exact subsection
   - Effective date and subsequent relevant amendment, when known
   - Explanation of how the code follows from the legal language and applicable coding rule
   - Manual-review status when uncertainty remains

2. **Historical sourcing.** Prefer official historical administrative codes and archived rules, followed by official rulemaking records and historical agency publications, then reliable legal reproductions and secondary sources when primary historical text is unavailable. Check the document's effective date against the observation date. Do not use a current regulation alone to establish a historical value. When a source is unavailable, record that limitation rather than presenting a substitute as the exact historical version without evidence.

3. **SCR and MGS documentation.** For fractional SCR, document the staffing arithmetic. For unrestricted MGS, document which provisions were inspected. Evaluate SCR and MGS independently under the applicable coding rules.

4. **Uncertainty.** Separate uncertainty about whether a source was legally effective on the observation date from uncertainty about how the legal language should be interpreted. Flag ambiguous cases for manual review rather than silently resolving them.

5. **Preserving existing codes and notes.** Preserve existing coded values and researcher notes. Propose corrections separately; never silently overwrite them.

These are reusable sourcing and checking instructions, **not a claim that one fixed prompt was used throughout the project**. They also do not replace manual legal review or constitute a record of a particular completed audit.
